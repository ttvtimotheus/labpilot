# Product

## Register

product

## Users

LabPilot is primarily for MTL students and people early in laboratory practice who need support while learning and performing routine lab workflows. It also serves beginner-to-intermediate laboratory staff who want a fast, reliable working aid for timers, protocol steps, counters, reference values, and quick repetition.

Users may be in training rooms, teaching labs, or real routine contexts where attention is divided between physical work, time-sensitive steps, documentation, and safety-critical habits. The interface must assume limited spare attention, small mobile screens, gloved or hurried interaction, and a need for immediate orientation.

## Product Purpose

LabPilot is an opinionated mobile laboratory companion for routine organization, learning, and quick reference. It brings timers, standard protocols, colony and differential counters, reference libraries, calculators, and learning cards into one offline-first workspace.

Success means users can move through common lab tasks with calm confidence: start timers quickly, count without distraction, open the right protocol or reference without digging, and learn the structure of standard workflows while doing the work. The product should feel premium-ready and professionally credible, while staying clear that it supports training and routine organization rather than validated medical decision-making.

## Brand Personality

LabPilot is precise, professional, and educational. It should feel calm, dense, and capable without becoming cold, academic, playful, or generic.

The tone should communicate expert restraint: short labels, clear hierarchy, no hype, no filler, and no visible technical scaffolding in everyday screens. It should make beginners feel oriented without talking down to them, and make routine users feel the app respects their time.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic dashboard UI: interchangeable cards, decorative metrics, fake activity, configurable blank-canvas layouts, and list rows that all feel the same.

Avoid Notion or Obsidian as a product metaphor. LabPilot is not a build-your-own workspace, note system, or empty sheet. It is a tool with a clear point of view.

Avoid fitness-app gamification such as streak badges, confetti, playful rewards, or motivational nudges. Lab work should not feel like MyFitnessPal, Lifesum, or a habit tracker.

Avoid academic medical-library interfaces such as dense PDF-like text walls or slow encyclopedia browsing. The knowledge bank should be fast and touchable, not a document archive.

Avoid an Android Material app look, including loud floating action buttons, overly colorful surfaces, excessive ripple choreography, and platform-specific compromises. The target feel is iOS-native clarity applied consistently across platforms.

Avoid technical or prototype language in primary surfaces: missing keys, backend configuration, placeholder content, demo data, and setup details belong only in contextual settings or diagnostic flows.

## Design Principles

1. Instrument before dashboard: every screen should feel like a working tool with a clear job, not a collection of widgets.

2. Dense without overload: use the Apple Health model of grouped, filterable, quickly scannable information so users can read more without feeling buried.

3. Tap means action: core workflows should respond immediately, with minimal confirmation steps and no hidden paths for common actions.

4. The UI gets out of the way during work: especially for counters and active timers, maximize target size, reduce chrome, and keep the current task dominant.

5. Teach through structure: help beginners understand routine workflows through clear grouping, vocabulary, and sequence, not through long explanatory copy.

6. Credibility over excitement: professional restraint, visible safety boundaries, and clear product limits matter more than delight, novelty, or animation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

LabPilot should meet WCAG 2.2 AA as a baseline and go beyond it where it improves real laboratory use. The product must support low cognitive load, strong contrast, visible focus, large tap targets, predictable reading order, and clear labels that remain visible.

The interface must not rely on color alone for state, area, urgency, or success. It should support reduced motion and high contrast or forced colors settings without losing meaning. Copy should be plain, respectful, and beginner-friendly while remaining professionally precise.

Because users may be new to laboratory practice, under time pressure, or working in distracting environments, error states and safety boundaries should be explicit, calm, and actionable. LabPilot must not present itself as a medical device, replace validated laboratory processes, or imply authority over diagnosis, reporting, or result release.
